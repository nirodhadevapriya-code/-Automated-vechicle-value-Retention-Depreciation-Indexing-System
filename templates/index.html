Our thinking

Buying a used car should not feel like guessing.

Two cars that look alike can be priced thousands of dollars apart. We built AutoWorth to give buyers, sellers and dealers a fair starting point based on evidence.

01

Price from evidence

Every estimate comes from patterns in 14,011 real listings, after we removed repeated and unrealistic ones.

02

Explain, not just predict

You can see which factors move a price the most, such as age, equipment, engine and gear box, and how each one behaves.

03

Be honest about doubt

No model is exact. Each estimate comes with a range that held the real price for 80% of cars we tested.

Model discussion

Four models, one honest comparison

We trained four different kinds of model on the same data, tuned them with a proper search, and chose the final one by a rule fixed in advance: the best cross-validated score on the training data. The test cars were kept aside for the final check.

Why XGBoost is the final model

It scored highest on the agreed measure (cross-validated R²) and also on the test cars, with the lowest average error. The saved model is small and answers in milliseconds.

An honest caveat

XGBoost, LightGBM and Random Forest are very close. The gap between them is smaller than the variation between folds, so we do not claim one is clearly better. All three are far ahead of the straight-line model, because prices depend on combinations of factors.

How we turned 14,011 real listings into a price estimator that tells you how sure it is.

The problem

Two similar cars, two very different prices.

Our solution

Tell us the car. We estimate the price and how sure we are.

Recommendations

Use the estimate to start a better conversation.

BuyersCompare the ask with the range. A price far below it deserves extra checks of the car's history.
SellersList close to the estimate. The range shows how much room there is to negotiate.
DealersUse it for quick trade-in values and look closely when the four models disagree.
